# Rounds — how a circle is asked

A round is one message. The human answers it by number, in any order and at any length.

## Layout

Your own thoughts first, when the round widens; then the questions.

```
**P1 — <title>.** <What you would add or change, why it is worth it, what it costs.>
→ Take it, leave it, or take it further?

**Q1 — <title>.** <The question. Options where there are real ones, with what follows
from each.>
→ <Your recommended answer, and why.>
```

Number proposals and questions separately and keep the numbers across the circle: "P2" in
round three is still the thought from round one.

## Widening

A widening round brings what the human did not say. Three directions; use the ones that
give something, at least two:

- **Wider** — what sits next to the idea: a neighbouring problem the same work would solve,
  another audience with the same need, something already planned here that it meets.
- **Deeper** — what becomes possible once it works: the next thing someone will ask for,
  what it lets the project stop doing.
- **Sideways** — another road to the same goal: a different form (a convention instead of a
  tool, a script instead of a service), the smallest version worth having, the idea turned
  inside out.

Build them from what §2 found — the code, the other specs, the queue, what others do —
not from the idea's own words. The test: could the human have written this thought
themselves from what they had already said? Then it widened nothing.

Widen less as the circles go on. The first circle looks mostly wider and sideways; a later
one, opened on a direction the human chose, looks mostly deeper into that direction.

## Narrowing

The **frontier** is every decision whose prerequisites are settled: what can be asked now
without guessing at an answer not heard yet. Ask all of it in one round. An answer moves
the frontier outward; recompute it before the next round. A fact still being looked up is
an unsettled prerequisite: only the questions that hang on it wait.

A proposal the human took becomes decisions to settle. One they left is a rejected option:
it goes to "Decisions" with their reason and is not offered again. One they liked and put
off goes to "Set aside".

## Closing a circle

```
**The idea now.** <One sentence.>

**What I would write down.**
- <Decision> — rejected: <option>, because <reason>.
- Open: <question> — what depends on it.
- Assumed: <what you took yourself instead of asking> — what would test it.

**Where else this could go.**
1. <A direction seen and not opened.>
2. …

Write it down, or another circle — on which of these?
```

List in the third part what you actually see, neither padded nor trimmed to look finished.
With nothing to list, say so.
